AI is giving us the illusion of intelligence. And combined with Dunning-Kruger syndrome, we're heading somewhere strange, the smart are doubting themselves more, and the clueless are more confident than ever.

Let me explain…

## The AK-47 at a paintball game

Anthropic, OpenAI, and other companies ship better models almost every month. Most people just grab the latest, most expensive one and throw it at everything. Summarizing a Slack thread? GPT-5. Renaming a variable? Claude Opus 4.7 in xhigh thinking mode. It's like bringing an AK-47 to a paintball game. Completely unnecessary and often dangerous.

Picking the right model is hard, sure. LLM companies default to the best one because it's safer. But that's exactly where individual accountability matters. Take some time. Self-learn. Read the docs. Understand what you're using.

The ignorant just keep being ignorant, happily, expensively, and now with confidence.

## AI as a shield

I've watched people use AI to dodge work. At a company I know, there's an internal wiki bot connected to all the developer docs and code. I saw two engineers literally trying to convince the bot that a support ticket wasn't their responsibility. Not solving it. Not escalating it. Negotiating with a chatbot to make it go away.

This is the new shape of avoidance. AI is the shield, the alibi, and the excuse, all in one.

## The "smart" trap

Here's the part that messes with me personally.

I was redesigning my personal website recently. Claude suggested that I use Astro. I didn't know what Astro was, so I asked. I got a very surface-level answer. I went ahead anyway. The site no doubt, turned out to be beautiful. I don't think I would ever be able to make such a website by myself without AI. But the point is…

I still don't know what Astro actually does.

The output looks like I'm smart. I'm not. The gap I had before AI is still there. AI just papered over it nicely. And every time this happens, my self-doubt grows. I scrutinize every line of AI output in case it screws up something I'm building, but I'm taking architectural guesses I can't fully defend. The smarter the tool, the dumber I feel.

The people with some accountability are doing this too, double-checking, second-guessing, and learning slowly. That's the right instinct, I guess. The self-doubt is correct, I hope.

## The bullshitters are winning

Meanwhile, people are bullshitting their way up the ladder and somehow surviving. I know a senior product leader, building AI products, who has never used Claude. Not once.

This shouldn't be possible. But it is. Because AI lets you sound smart in meetings without being smart. You can ship a deck, a doc, a strategy memo, and never have done the underlying work. Dunning-Kruger on steroids: confident, articulate, fluent, and hollow.

## And the real world is on fire

Layered on top of all this: gen AI images and videos are now indistinguishable from real ones. Try convincing your parents that a video is fake. Try convincing yourself, six months from now. It's only getting harder.

We're losing the ability to tell what's real, while simultaneously losing the ability to tell who's actually competent.

## So where does this leave us?

The careful are getting more careful. The clueless are getting more confident. The output of both groups looks roughly the same on the surface, polished, articulate, AI-shaped.

We're collectively getting dumber while feeling smarter.

The only defense is the unsexy one: take accountability. Learn the thing. Read the docs. Use the smaller model to change the variable. Question the output. Notice when AI is filling a gap you should be filling yourself.

The illusion of intelligence is comfortable. Real intelligence rarely is.
